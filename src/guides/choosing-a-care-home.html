Family Guide

How to Choose the Right Care Home for Your Parent

Choosing a care home is one of the biggest decisions a family makes, and it often has to happen quickly. This guide walks you through it step by step: what to look for, what to ask, and how to check a home before you decide.

Last updated November 2026

1. Start with the care your parent needs

Before you look at homes, write down the help your parent needs every day. This decides which homes can care for them safely.

  • Daily help: bathing, dressing, eating, using the toilet, walking or moving from bed to chair
  • Medicines: how many, how often, and whether someone needs to give them
  • Memory: forgetfulness, confusion, wandering or changes in behavior
  • Medical care: wounds, injections, oxygen or other nursing care
  • Daily life: language, food, faith, routines and the people they want nearby

Not sure where to start? Our free Care Assessment takes two minutes and gives you a starting point.

2. Know the main types of care homes

In California, most care homes for older adults are licensed by the California Department of Social Services as Residential Care Facilities for the Elderly (RCFEs). They come in different sizes:

  • Small board and care homes: usually six residents or fewer, in a house in a neighborhood. Care is personal, and staff get to know each resident well.
  • Assisted living communities: larger buildings with more residents, activities and amenities.
  • Memory care: homes or units for people living with dementia, with extra safety features and specially trained staff.

If your parent needs nursing or medical care around the clock, a skilled nursing facility may be the better fit. Those are licensed by the California Department of Public Health.

3. Set a budget and look at ways to pay

Costs depend on the area, the size of the home, the room (private or shared) and how much care your parent needs. Ask every home exactly what the monthly price includes and what costs extra.

Most families pay privately. Other help can include:

  • Long-term care insurance, if your parent has a policy
  • VA Aid and Attendance, for eligible veterans and surviving spouses
  • Medi-Cal's Assisted Living Waiver, which helps pay for care (not room and board) in some counties and often has a waiting list

4. Check the home's license and inspection history

Every care home for older adults in California must be licensed by the state. Before you visit, look the home up on the state's free Care Facility Search website. You can see:

  • Whether the license is active, and how many residents the home may care for
  • Reports from state inspections and complaint visits
  • Any citations, which is what the state calls a rule the home broke

One small citation is not unusual. A pattern of serious ones, especially about care, safety or staffing, is a warning sign. Ask the home what happened and what has changed since.

5. Visit, and visit again

A visit tells you things a website cannot. If you can, go more than once, at different times of day, and try to see a meal.

What to look for

  • Residents who are clean, dressed and engaged, not left alone for long periods
  • Staff who are kind and patient and know residents by name
  • A clean home without strong smells, with safe floors, grab bars and good lighting
  • Food that looks good, with choices for special diets

What to ask

  • How many caregivers are on duty during the day and at night?
  • What training do your caregivers have, especially for dementia?
  • Who gives medicines, and how do you prevent mistakes?
  • What happens if my parent falls, gets sick or needs to go to the hospital?
  • If my parent's needs grow, can they stay here? Can they receive hospice care here?
  • How do you keep families informed?

6. Read the admission agreement before you sign

Before your parent moves in, the home will ask you to sign an admission agreement. Take it home and read it first. Check:

  • The monthly rate, and exactly what it includes
  • Extra charges for more care, and how the level of care is decided
  • The refund policy if your parent moves out or passes away
  • When the home can ask your parent to move out, and how much notice it must give

Under California law, most care homes must give residents written notice at least 90 days before raising their rates, with the reason for the increase.

7. Know who to call if something goes wrong

California's Long-Term Care Ombudsman program helps people who live in care homes, free of charge. If you are worried about your parent's care or safety, call the 24-hour CRISISline at 1-800-231-4024. Every licensed home must post the contact details of its local ombudsman.
