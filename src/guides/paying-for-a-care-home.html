Family Guide

How to Pay for a Care Home in California

Care homes cost a lot, and the rules about who pays are confusing. This guide explains, step by step, what each program does and does not pay for, so you can plan with fewer surprises.

Last updated November 2026

1. Know what the monthly price covers

Most care homes charge a monthly rate for the room and meals, plus a care fee that depends on how much help your parent needs. Ask each home for a written list of what is included and what costs extra:

  • The room and meals, and whether the room is private or shared
  • Help with bathing, dressing, medicines and getting around
  • Laundry, activities and rides to appointments
  • How the care fee is decided, and when it can go up

Our Cost Calculator shows a typical monthly range for your area.

2. What Medicare pays for, and what it does not

Medicare does not pay for room and board or for daily personal care in a care home. Many families are surprised by this.

Medicare still pays for your parent's doctor visits, hospital care and covered medicines. It also pays for hospice care, which can be given in a care home that is allowed to provide it.

3. Medi-Cal and the Assisted Living Waiver

Medi-Cal does not pay for room and board in a care home either. But in some counties, its Assisted Living Waiver can pay for the care part of the cost for people who qualify:

  • It is available in 15 counties, including Los Angeles, Orange, San Diego, Riverside and San Bernardino
  • Your parent must qualify for Medi-Cal and need the level of care a nursing home gives
  • The care home must take part in the program
  • Your parent still pays for room and board, usually from their own income
  • There is often a long waiting list, so apply early

Since January 2026, Medi-Cal for people 65 and older has a limit on savings and other assets again. The home your parent lives in, one car and household items usually do not count. Ask your county social services office for the current limit before you apply.

Read about the Assisted Living Waiver on the Medi-Cal website

4. SSI for people with low income

If your parent receives SSI (Supplemental Security Income), the monthly payment is higher while they live in a licensed care home, because California adds more for care home residents. Not every home accepts residents who pay with SSI, so ask each home.

5. Veterans and surviving spouses

Wartime veterans and their surviving spouses with limited income and savings may get a VA pension. An extra monthly amount, called Aid and Attendance, can be added for people who:

  • Need help with daily activities such as bathing, dressing or eating
  • Are bedridden, or live in a nursing home
  • Have very limited eyesight

The need for help does not have to be caused by military service. A doctor fills in VA Form 21-2680 about the care your parent needs. The amounts change every December.

Read about Aid and Attendance on the VA website

6. Long-term care insurance

If your parent has a long-term care insurance policy, call the insurance company early. Ask:

  • Does the policy pay for care in a residential care home or assisted living?
  • How much does it pay per day or per month, and for how long?
  • Is there a waiting period before payments start?
  • What forms and doctor's reports do you need?

Most policies start paying when a doctor confirms your parent needs help with at least two daily activities, such as bathing or dressing, or has serious memory loss.

7. Other ways families pay

  • Your parent's savings, pension and Social Security income
  • Selling or renting out your parent's home
  • Family members sharing the cost
  • Some life insurance policies, which can pay part of the benefit early in some situations

Before you move or give away money or property so that your parent qualifies for a program, get advice. It can affect their eligibility.

8. Get free help, and watch out for fees

HICAP, California's free Health Insurance Counseling and Advocacy Program, can explain Medicare, Medi-Cal and long-term care insurance. Its counselors do not sell insurance. Call 1-800-434-0222 to find your local office.

For VA benefits, your county veterans service office can help you apply for free. No one is allowed to charge you for help filing a first VA pension claim. Be careful with anyone who asks for a fee or tells you to move money so that your parent qualifies.
